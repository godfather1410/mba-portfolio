# Zwigato Delivery: Bayes' Theorem vs Monte Carlo Simulation

**Course:** Statistical Methods for Decision Making (SMDM-1)  
**Programme:** PGPM 2026-27, Great Lakes Institute of Management, Term 1 (May-Jul 2026)  
**Type:** Individual  
**Skills:** Conditional probability, Bayes' rule, Monte Carlo simulation in Excel

## The question / brief
_Summarised from the course brief._

Zwigato (food delivery): 10% of orders are delayed; 70% of delayed orders lead to a complaint; on-time orders also get complaints at a rate set by your roll number (10% here).

Manager's question: 'To what extent does a customer complaint indicate that an order was actually delayed?'

Tasks: compute P(Delay | Complaint) with Bayes' rule, simulate delivery outcomes and complaints (repeated random trials), estimate the probability empirically, show how the estimate converges as trials grow, and connect the result to a managerial decision.


## What I did
Estimated P(Delay | Complaint) for a food-delivery platform analytically with Bayes' theorem (0.4375) and empirically with Excel RAND() simulations of 100, 1,000 and 5,000 orders, showing how the relative frequency converges to the theoretical value and what a complaint really tells a manager about delays.
