**Company:** RetailCorp Inc.  **Fiscal year:** FY2025
**Footprint:** 340 brick-and-mortar stores, mid-tier apparel, home goods and seasonal,
primarily in regional enclosed shopping malls

## Benchmark gaps

| Metric | RetailCorp | Industry norm | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| E-commerce share of net revenue | 12.0% ($49.4M) | 34% | **-22 pp** |
| Inventory turnover | 3.1x | 4.0x to 5.0x | -0.9x below range floor |
| Enclosed-mall foot traffic | -18% YoY | mid-single-digit sector decline | roughly 3x the sector rate |
| Digital share of marketing and capex budget | not disclosed | 25% to 35% | not derivable |

The channel gap of 22 pp is past the 15 pp line in the benchmarks file, so it is carried into the
threat ranking as a structural position rather than a tactical one.

## Competitor strategy

- OmniRetail and TrendX each reallocated 30% of FY2025 marketing and capital expenditure toward
  digital channels and same-day local fulfilment. That sits inside the 25-35% sector norm, which
  makes RetailCorp the outlier on channel investment rather than the competitors being aggressive.
- Share is being lost in two directions at once: to direct-to-consumer brands and to big-box
  discount retailers. An estimated 2.8% of market share in apparel and home goods went in FY2025.
  The document gives this as an estimate and it is treated as one here.

## Channel shift

- The 340-store estate is concentrated in exactly the format that is declining. Enclosed-mall
  locations lost 18% of foot traffic year on year, against a sector that is drifting down at mid
  single digits.
- E-commerce reached $49.4M, 12% of net revenue, against an industry average of 34%. On the
  document's own comparator the company is running roughly a third of the sector's digital mix.

## Consumer demand

- **The segment that works:** regional stores in high-density suburban strip hubs grew sales 2.1%
  and hold strong repeat-customer retention. This is the only growing line in the document and is
  the obvious base for a turnaround plan to build on.
- Demand has not disappeared, it has moved format and channel. The strip-hub result and the
  e-commerce shortfall point at the same conclusion from opposite directions.

## Top three threats to market share

1. **Digital channel deficit, 22 pp below the industry norm.** The largest single gap in the
   report and the one competitors are actively widening.
2. **Format concentration in enclosed malls.** 340 stores sit in a format losing traffic at 18% a
   year. This is a fixed-cost exposure, not just a sales one.
3. **Two-sided share loss to DTC and discounters.** Being undercut on price and out-served on
   convenience at the same time leaves the mid-tier position without a defensible edge.
