## Diagnosis

RetailCorp is not failing because it cannot sell; it is failing because 340 of its stores sit in a
format its customers are leaving, and it has no digital channel to catch them in. That strategic
problem has now become a financing problem: EBITDA fell 34.1% in one year, which pushed
debt-service coverage to 1.15x against a 1.10x covenant floor, with $12.5M falling due on
30 June 2026 against $13.8M of cash.

## Financial position

Revenue fell 8.4% to $412.0M and gross margin lost 4.2 percentage points to 34.0%, an 11.0%
relative decline. EBITDA fell further and faster, down 34.1% to $28.0M, taking the EBITDA margin
from 9.44% to 6.80%. The balance sheet moved with it: the current ratio fell through its floor to
1.10, working capital dropped 68.8% to $5.3M, and cash fell 37.3% to $13.8M. Total debt stands at
$97.5M, or 3.48x EBITDA. Three of seven measured ratios breached their thresholds.

## Market position

The company's e-commerce channel reaches 12% of revenue against an industry average of 34%, a gap
of 22 percentage points, while OmniRetail and TrendX have each moved 30% of their budgets toward
digital and same-day fulfilment. Enclosed-mall foot traffic fell 18% year on year, roughly three
times the sector rate, and an estimated 2.8% of market share went to DTC brands and discounters.

One line is growing. Regional stores in high-density suburban strip hubs grew 2.1% with strong
repeat retention. Demand has not gone; it has changed format.

## Decision matrix

| | Option A: Cost reduction and footprint rationalisation | Option B: Digital pivot | Option C: Refinance first |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | Store closure costs, not quantified in source | $3.0M CAPEX over 12 months | Refinancing fees, not quantified in source |
| **Time to impact** | 12 to 24 months (lease exits) | 12 months to platform, longer to 34% | Immediate on the June 2026 maturity |
| **Addresses root cause** | Partly. Cuts exposure to the dying format, does not create a new channel | Yes. Closes the 22 pp channel gap | No. Buys time, changes nothing operationally |
| **Effect on covenant** | Improves coverage slowly; closure costs may worsen it first | Neutral to negative near term | Directly addresses the binding constraint |
| **Risk if it fails** | Cuts into the strip-hub segment that is still growing | $3.0M spent with the maturity still unresolved | Refinanced on worse terms, with the underlying decline untouched |
| **Evidence base** | 18% traffic decline, 340-store estate | 22 pp gap, competitor reallocation | 1.15x DSCR, $12.5M due 30 Jun 2026 |

## Recommendation

Sequence, do not choose. The three options address different time horizons and only one of them
has a date attached.

1. **Refinance first (Option C), starting now.** The June 2026 maturity is the only item in the
   register with a fixed date and no discretion over timing. It should be addressed before the
   covenant is tested, not after, because coverage at 1.15x is the weakest negotiating position
   the company will have.
2. **Fund the digital pivot (Option B) in parallel.** At $3.0M it sits inside delegated authority
   and it is the only option that closes the gap the competitors are widening. Anchor it on the
   strip-hub segment that is already growing 2.1% rather than on the declining mall estate.
3. **Rationalise the footprint (Option A) once refinancing is secured.** Closure costs would
   worsen coverage in the near term, which is exactly the wrong order while the covenant is tight.

## Approval gate

No item in this report requires human confirmation under the $5.0M threshold. The largest costed
recommendation is the $3.0M digital CAPEX. If the pivot is rescoped above $5.0M, or if
refinancing fees take the total package past that line, the recommendation stops for human
confirmation before it is finalised.

## Limitations

- The market analysis is document-only. Competitor financials would change the confidence on the
  benchmarking, not the direction of it.
- Inventory, interest expense, net income, total assets and equity are absent from the source, so
  quick ratio, interest cover, ROA and ROE could not be computed and are not estimated.
- The document reports the liquidity decline as 18.5% and the gross margin change as "-4.2%".
  Computed from the underlying figures these are 18.73% and 4.2 percentage points respectively.
  The underlying figures were used throughout.
- Costs for Options A and C are not quantified in the source and are not invented here.
